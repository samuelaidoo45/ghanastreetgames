## Sulia for Beatings — rules unverified

Four Corners visibly lists it as a forthcoming mode; that establishes the label, not a ruleset. Do not equate it with keep-away, Zanzama, or Alikoto without evidence. ## Shooting / Pampanaa / Panpanaa — documented core, uncertain alias

Alex Blege's first-person recollection of childhood in Teshie Zongo describes at least four children split into two teams. Players hide, a call begins the round, and opponents search for each other. A spotted opponent is called out through pretend shooting with fingers or toy props. His account does not supply formal elimination, scoring, respawn or timed-match rules. Those would be digital design choices.

Graphic's 2024 report independently describes Pampanaa as a hide-and-spot pretend-shooting game. This supports the game, but not the claim that every Ghanaian use of “Shooting” means Pampanaa. Keep the alias confidence explicit. Panpanaa is another attested spelling. Do not confuse it with Pempenaa, the musical elimination game.

Sources:
- Alex Blege, 4 August 2015, first-person recollection: https://www.modernghana.com/news/634237/stroke-of-my-pen-playing-panpanaa-with-our-president.html
- Graphic Showbiz, 20 March 2024: https://www.graphic.com.gh/entertainment/showbiz-news/here-are-some-classic-local-games-from-back-in-the-day.html

## Dame / Damii — use a Ghanaian rules variant

The ordinary Ghanaian board is 10×10 with 20 pieces per side. Men move one diagonal square forward and can capture backward. Kings fly along diagonals. Captures are compulsory; the player may choose a capture route but must finish its available chain. A side left with just one piece loses. These are not English 8×8 checkers rules. Sources describe the board as mirrored relative to international draughts; preserve the selected board orientation consistently.

ArtSoftGH's own Dame app listing corroborates 10×10 and describes Kwakwa: forfeiting a king that neglects a capture or fails to complete its jumps. A client that only permits legal capture sequences need not implement an opportunity to make that forbidden move. Clearly identify any simpler school-board variant as an adaptation. Promotion and draw conventions should be specified: one documented implementation promotes only when the completed move ends on the last rank and draws after 25 king-only non-capture moves.

Sources:
- Ghanaian implementation and rule explanation: https://dammee.com/ (search index readable; direct fetch returned 403)
- Developer's listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_IN&id=com.artsoftgh.dame
- Rule reference: https://draughtsgame.com/en/rules/ghanaian-draughts

Confidence: high on the core board/capture differences, medium on universally shared draw conventions; local conventions may vary.

## Ludo — conventional core, explicit house rules

Funskool's published instructions support four pawns per player, six to enter the track, another throw after a six, returning an opposing pawn to base when landed on, and an exact roll to reach home. The first player to bring every pawn home wins. That source does not establish a Ghana-specific national ruleset or require optional safe squares, capture bonuses, blockades, or a three-sixes penalty. Pick and display the project's conventions. If using fewer tokens or a shortened route, call it a quick arcade variant.

Manufacturer instructions: https://funskoolindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Ludo_Instructions.pdf

Confidence: high for the conventional rules described; unverified as one universal Ghanaian ruleset.
