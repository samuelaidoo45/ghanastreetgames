# Ghana Street Games: research catalogue

Research checked 31 October 2026. This is a starting catalogue, not an exhaustive inventory of every Ghanaian game. Many sources document remembered childhood practices, not nationwide participation today. Names, songs, penalties and scoring differ between communities. “Played in Ghana” does not imply “originated in Ghana”.

## The three requested names

- **Stay / “Stay for you”:** Stay is documented in academic research and Ghanaian reporting. The exact phrase “Stay for you” was not independently established as a canonical title. Owusu and Obuo Addo describe juggling totals, a retriever/chaser, a call to stay, and returning the ball without moving. A University of Ghana thesis describes a freeze-and-throw variation targeting hands. Treat these as distinct accounts, not a single agreed ruleset. Sources A, J, K.
- **Shooting:** Graphic documents **Pampanaa**, a pretend shooting game involving hiding and spotting opponents with stick props. It is a plausible match, but “Shooting = Pampanaa” remains unconfirmed. Do not silently relabel one as the other. Source B.
- **Sulia for beatings:** Searches for the exact phrase, Sulia + Ghana + game/children, and variants did not locate a credible account of its rules. No inference that it is Zanzama, Alikoto or Kpitinge is justified. Keep the user-provided name, mark rules **unverified**, and await community evidence before implementing.

## Catalogue

Confidence describes documentation, not the existence of a single national ruleset. High = academic/official evidence or corroboration; medium = a described local/recollection account.

| Game | Brief description | Confidence and source |
|---|---|---|
| Street football / Four Corners | Football with improvised playing spaces; the referenced browser game offers three-a-side and a four-goal mode. | High; L; digital rules belong to that implementation. |
| Ampe | Partners clap, jump and choose a leading foot; agreed matching or opposing foot patterns determine the score. | High; C. |
| Pilolo | A leader conceals sticks; players search and return to a designated point, with scoring agreed beforehand. | High; A. Some recollections eliminate the shortest-stick finder (F, I). |
| Chaskele | A defender strikes an incoming object away from a tyre or container target. | High; B, I. Equipment and teams vary. |
| Oware | Players sow seeds through two rows of pits and compete to capture more than their opponent. | High; A. Specify the rules variant when implementing. |
| Alikoto / Alokoto | An improvised spinning top must land or be turned upright; historical accounts include hand-striking penalties. | High; B, F. Digital adaptation can use points. |
| Counters Ball | Bottle caps represent footballers, nudging a small ball toward improvised goals. | High; B, F. |
| Tumatu / Tuumatu / Tumantu | Hop through a marked ground pattern, avoiding occupied spaces and lines; local versions differ. | High; A. |
| Antoakyire / Antoekyire | A cloth is secretly left behind a seated circle member, prompting pursuit and a change of place. | High; B. |
| Kpitinge / Kpitingε / Kpiting3 | Players try to plant an object upright in sand; old accounts describe penalties for failed attempts. | High; B, F. |
| Pampanaa / Pampana | Hide-and-spot pretend shooting using improvised props; a childhood role-play game. | Medium; B. Do not confuse with Pempenaa. |
| Ludo / Ludu | Players roll a die and race their tokens from their starting area around a board. | High for Ghanaian play; B. Not exclusively Ghanaian. |
| Stay | Ball-control, chasing and freezing appear in documented versions; the call and allowed return differ. | High; A, J, K. |
| Tyre rolling | Children propel a tyre or wheel rim with a stick, keeping it upright while moving or racing. | Medium; G. |
| Skipping rope | Individual or group rope jumping, often accompanied by a repeated song or chant and shared rhythm. | Medium; G. |
| Dame | A strategy game named in Ghanaian childhood accounts and field research; publish a specified local ruleset before play. | High for name/presence; G, H. Detailed rules not verified here. |
| Maame ne Paapa | Imaginative family play recreates caregiving and household roles, sometimes using dolls; there is no fixed competitive ruleset. | Medium; F. |
| Practical Numbers | A remembered turn-taking number challenge requires a quick correct prime-number response to remain in the round. | Medium; F. This is one reported version. |
| Zanzama / Zanzamaa | Players remember to announce the name before sitting; historical accounts describe a head-knock for forgetting. | High; F, I. For adaptation, substitute points. |
| Chempε / Chem Pe | A food-sharing pact requires an agreed declaration; forgetting it can trigger equal sharing among participating players. | High; I, A. |
| Alasa | Players flick fruit seeds toward a small hole, competing on accuracy from an agreed distance. | Medium; I. |
| Kyekyekule / Kyerε Wo Ti | A leader names or indicates body parts, and the circle follows the actions within a musical exchange. | High; C. |
| Pempenaa | Seated players extend their legs; a leader’s song determines which leg withdraws until the round resolves. | High; C. Distinct from Pampanaa. |
| Mbobagor | Players pass stones around a circle to song; accumulating stones in front of one player eliminates them. | High; D, a Central Region account. |
| Sansankroma | One documented version casts a child as a hawk chasing others toward refuges; other sources attach the song to stone-passing. | High; C, D. Preserve variant distinction. |

## Editorial and implementation guidance

Distinguish **playable digital adaptation**, **documented game**, **documented name but rules incomplete**, and **unverified local name**. Do not market this catalogue as “all Ghanaian games”. Include imported games widely played in Ghana without claiming Ghanaian invention. Preserve local spellings as aliases and avoid assigning an ethnic group or region without evidence. Label a simplified game’s rule changes. Folk practices involving hitting can be described historically; use score changes in this children's arcade rather than teaching physical punishments. Further community validation should collect the town, language, name, equipment, scoring, role changes, and consented demonstration.
